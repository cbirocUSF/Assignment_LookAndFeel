How to Read These Numbers

The first two charts describe the ten named scholarship programs in our catalog, which commit $3.22 million per application cycle. The third chart describes all student aid from every source, which reached $39.7 million in 2026 and also includes federal Pell grants, student loans, state aid, tuition waivers and graduate assistantship stipends. The scholarship catalog is therefore a subset of total aid, accounting for roughly 8% of it. The two figures measure different things in a second way as well: the catalog counts dollars committed for one cycle, while the trend line counts dollars actually disbursed in a calendar year.

Dollars Committed by Category

Award amount multiplied by the number of awards offered in each category. Need-based aid carries the largest commitment because it reaches the most students.

Share of Awards by Category

Counted by individual awards rather than dollars, which shows how many students each category actually reaches.

Total Student Aid Disbursed, All Sources, 2020 to 2026

Every dollar of student aid disbursed in each year, in millions. This is a wider measure than the two charts above: alongside the named scholarship catalog it includes federal grants and loans, state aid, tuition waivers and assistantship stipends. Total aid has more than doubled since 2020, and the $3.22 million scholarship catalog is about 8% of the 2026 figure.

Finding 1

Breadth beats size

Need-based aid has the second smallest award at $6,500, but with 120 awards it commits more money than any other category and reaches the most students.

Finding 2

Research funding is concentrated

The graduate research fellowship is the largest single award at $22,000, but only twelve are offered, so it is a small share of the overall portfolio.

Finding 3

Steady growth

Total student aid has risen every year since 2020 without a single flat or down year, which suggests the commitment is budgeted rather than opportunistic.
